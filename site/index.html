How it works

It starts with your call and stops when it ends

1

It notices the call

When Zoom, Meet or Teams turns on your mic, Earshot asks if it should start. One click, and it hears both sides: you through the mic, them through your Mac's sound.

2

It helps while you talk

Speech becomes text on your Mac. Earshot checks your notes on this person or project and nudges you when it helps. Press ⌘B when you wish you'd had help.

3

It writes the notes and remembers

When the call ends, Earshot writes the notes in your format. It saves what it learned, so the next call starts where this one left off.

Topics that learn

A memory for every deal, person and project

Each deal, person or project gets a folder of notes, called a topic. Every call on it makes it smarter.

  • One page of what's true now. After every call, Earshot rewrites the topic's memory: people, numbers, decisions, open to-dos and what changed.
  • It learns what you needed. Every time you press ⌘B, the memory notes what to have ready next time.
  • It stays small. Before a call, the AI reads the memory and the last three calls, not every call ever. A topic with 300 calls costs about the same as one with 3.
  • Plain text files on your Mac. Edit them, open them in Obsidian, or point an AI agent at them.
Notes your way

Notes in the format each meeting needs

A sales call, a weekly team meeting and a one-to-one need different notes. Type the headings you want, or reuse a skill: the instruction file you already give Claude Code or another AI agent.

  • Shared meeting notes, drafted for you. Connect a topic to a page in Spacesheep, Notion or another tool. After the call, Earshot drafts this week's entry for that page.
  • Nothing goes out without you. The draft waits for you to read it. Edit it, then press Publish.
  • Your team sees it where they already work. The notes land on the shared page, with every version kept, where people and their AI agents can read them.

Private by design

Your calls stay on your Mac

There is no Earshot server. There's no account to create, and nothing to sign up for.

Audio never leavesRecorded and transcribed on your Mac, and deleted after 14 days unless you choose otherwise.
Files you ownTranscripts, notes and memory are plain markdown in a folder you choose.
Only what you sendText goes only to the model you picked. With Ollama or LM Studio, nothing leaves at all.
Keys in the keychainAPI keys and connector sign-ins are kept in the macOS keychain. Logs never contain what was said.
Questions

Good to know

Is it really free?

Yes. Earshot is open source under the MIT licence. You pay your model provider directly if you use a cloud model, or nothing at all with a local one through Ollama or LM Studio.

Does a bot join my meetings?

No. Earshot runs on your Mac and hears the call the way you do, through your mic and your system audio. Nobody else on the call sees anything join. Some places require everyone on a call to agree to recording, so ask first.

Which Macs does it run on?

Apple silicon Macs with macOS 26 or later. It uses Apple's newest speech and audio APIs, which arrived in macOS 26.

macOS says it can't open Earshot. What do I do?

The app isn't notarised by Apple yet. After dragging it into Applications, open System Settings → Privacy & Security and click Open Anyway, or run xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/Earshot.app once.

Can I use my Claude or ChatGPT subscription?

Earshot can run the claude, codex and kiro-cli tools you already have installed, with your own login. Check each provider's terms before using a subscription this way. API keys and local models are always fine.

How is this different from other meeting notetakers?

Most notetakers focus on the write-up. Earshot's focus is helping during the call, with nudges drawn from your own notes. It's also fully local and open source, uses the models you choose, and keeps everything as markdown you own.

Who built it?

Earshot is a community project on GitHub. Its code and docs were written with AI and have not been reviewed by a human, so read the code before you trust it with your calls. Bug reports and pull requests are welcome.
